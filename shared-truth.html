Truth survives the trip between language models, right up until the last step

Shared Truth: Emergent Truth Properties in Large Language Models via Heterogeneous Injection-based Transfer

  • Isaiah Freeman, LinkedIn profile (opens in a new tab)
  • Joed Ngangmeni, LinkedIn profile (opens in a new tab)

Department of Computer Science, Virginia State University

Trained adapters can pass hidden states from one language model to another, even across model families. Does a specific concept such as truth survive that transfer? We map a source model's activations into a target model's representation space, blend them in at an injection strength α from 0 to 1, and read the result with the target's own truth probe. We repeat this across 21 cross-family trajectories built from Llama, Gemma, Qwen and Mistral models.

The target's ranking of true over false statements stays near its native ceiling through most of the sweep and collapses only at full replacement. Most of the apparent accuracy gain under partial injection is threshold recalibration. The collapse comes from the learned adapter rather than from swapping models: a closed-form rigid map keeps median AUROC at 0.91 where the trained adapter falls to 0.72. In practice, full substitution through a learned adapter should be treated as a deployment boundary.

01 · The methodIn the player

Reading one model’s activations with another model’s truth probe

A source model’s activation is mapped by a learned adapter, mixed into the target’s own activation at strength α, and scored by the target’s native truth probe, one held-out statement at a time.

02 · Fifty-three statementsIn the player

Truth stays readable until the very last step

True and false statements stay apart through most of the injection sweep, start to slide after α = 0.8, and pile into the middle only at full replacement.

03 · Twenty-one trajectoriesIn the player

Rank order holds across 21 cross-family pairs

On every source → target pair, AUROC sits near its native ceiling through α = 0.7 while accuracy at a fixed threshold drifts first; at full injection, median AUROC falls to 0.72 and separability drops about 14×.

04 · The accuracy overshootIn the player

Most of the accuracy “gain” is a better-placed threshold

Partial injection can lift accuracy above native levels, but re-tuning the threshold at each α shows most of that lift is recalibration, leaving a smaller real separability gain (median +1.13 pp).

05 · Two alignment mapsIn the player

The collapse belongs to the learned adapter, not the model swap

At full replacement, a closed-form rigid Procrustes map keeps median AUROC at 0.91, against 0.72 for the trained adapter, and does better on all 21 pairs.

06 · Residual geometryIn the player

The truth signal survives, but drifts off the probe’s axis

Read along the native truth direction, adapter output at α = 1 loses most of its separation, yet a fresh probe trained on the same output recovers 0.84 to 0.95 AUROC: the information is still there, only misaligned.
